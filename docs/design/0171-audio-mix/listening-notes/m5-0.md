# M5-0 listening section: the stamped bus path (ADR 0171 amended by 0172, task LS0)

The owner's section for milestone M5-0: bus changes that land on a **stamp** (a frame the core was told ahead of time) instead of "as soon as the next block comes", a presence duck that starts on **the sound's own frame**, and a reverb send that **goes quiet with its bus's fader** (post-fader). It is the first of three M5 sections (M5b loudness and M5c live edit follow; Ls1 joins them into `listening-pass.md`).

It was written from the running Sound Lab, with its headless walker beside it (`tests/e2e/audio-mix-listening.spec.ts`), not from the design. The numbers below are what the walker reads in the page. **Nobody has auditioned this yet:** every "correct" line is what the engine is built to do and the walker's meters and lane values agree with, not a finding. Mark each step **heard OK** or **issue** (with a note) in the sign-off block at the end.

## What you need

- **Headphones**, wired if you can (a Bluetooth output adds 100 ms or more and blurs "on the frame"). Chromium (Chrome or Edge).
- The repository with M5-0 built (`npm install` once, then `npm run test:e2e:install` for the walker).

### Run it

```bash
npm run dev
```

Open `http://localhost:3000/editor.html?sample=sound-lab`, press **Play**, and click the game view once (sound starts after a gesture, and the click gives the game the keys). Keys go to the game view, so keep it focused. **Window > Audio Debug** shows the bus rows (effective gain and a peak meter per bus) if you want to watch while you listen.

To let the walker prompt you step by step and check each step afterwards (it waits for your key, reads the page, and says in the terminal which step ran as built):

```bash
E2E_LISTEN=1 npm run test:e2e:slow -- audio-mix-listening --headed --workers 1 -g "M5-0 \(owner"
```

A card over the page lists the keys; the terminal prints `[listen] M5-0: press N ...` with what to listen for. The spec never times out while it waits for you.

The same walk without you (the headless check; it proves the page does what the steps say, not how it sounds):

```bash
npm run test:e2e:slow -- audio-mix-listening --project chromium-slow --workers 1
```

It has two cases: `M5-0 (owner or headless)` (S0 to S5 below, pressing the keys itself) and `M5-0 (listen mode)` (the same keys at human pace: seconds between keys, one key late, one key twice, one key held; step S6 below).

## The test material (what each key plays)

A sine hides a post-fader mute, a duck or a dip, and a steady tone gives no instant to land a stamp on, so the page uses broadband, percussive material. Everything is on the Sound Lab main page; nothing else is edited.

| Key | Does | Material | Bus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | pauses or resumes the Music tone (the page's own background loop) | `music-loop.wav`, a steady tone | Music |
| **B** | starts or stops the drum loop | `drum-loop.wav` (added for this section): one bar at 120 bpm, kick on 1 and 3, noise snare on 2 and 4, noise hats on every eighth; every hit starts on its grid sample | Music |
| **A** | starts or stops a looping noise | `noise-loop.wav`, broadband | Ambience (sends to the reverb) |
| **3** | fades Music to a quarter over 0.5 s; pressed again, back up | | Music |
| **4** | the Underwater snapshot on; pressed again, off | the project's snapshot: Music volume 0.6 and low-pass 500 Hz, Ambience volume 0.5 and low-pass 900 Hz, SFX low-pass 1500 Hz | all |
| **5** | a 0.3 s noise burst now | `burst.wav` | SFX |
| **X** | the same burst, scheduled **1 s after the key** | `burst.wav` with `at` | SFX |
| **2** | mutes or unmutes Music | | Music |
| **N** | mutes or unmutes Ambience | | Ambience |
| **Z** | fades Ambience to a quarter over 0.5 s and back (not used by a step below; there to try) | | Ambience |

The page also plays the project's **hum** on Ambience from the start (orbiting around you). It is the quiet constant under every step; it is muted along with Ambience in step S5.

The project's rules these steps use: Music's authored volume is 0.6; the ducking rule is Music ducked by SFX, mode presence, **amount 0.3, attack 0.05 s, release 0.4 s**; Ambience has a reverb send of 0.5.

## Setup (S0, do it once)

Press **P** (the tone pauses) and **B** (the drums start). You should hear a clean drum bar looping at 120 bpm with the hum faintly behind it. If the drums are not there, wait a second (the clip decodes on its first play) and press B again only if Audio Debug shows no `drum-loop` voice on Music.

**Walker:** the `drum-loop` voice is playing on Music and Music's meter is above 0.02.

## The steps

Each step starts from the setup state (drums on, everything else at rest). Do them in order the first time; each one leaves the page as it found it.

### S1. A scripted fade lands on its stamp. Key **3**, then **3** again

- **Press 3 and listen.** Correct: the drums get quieter in one smooth slope over half a second, starting a fraction of a second after the key (the engine stamps it a little ahead so it can start on an exact frame), ending at a quarter of the level (0.6 x 0.25 = 0.15). It is a plain level change: no click when it starts, no steps or graininess on the way (zipper), no jump at the end. The hum does not move.
- **Press 3 again.** Correct: the same slope up to the full 0.6; the drums are back at the level they had.
- **A defect sounds like:** a click at the start of the slope; a staircase or buzz during it; the whole change as one jump (the fade collapsed to a step); the fade starting late enough that the key feels broken (over about a third of a second); the drums not returning to the original level.
- **Walker confirms (not by ear):** Music's row shows a stamped ramp whose frame is ahead of the core's frame at the key and within half a second of it; one `BUS_RAMP` on Music's runtime lane (gain 0.25, 0.5 s) carrying that same stamp, and nothing re-sent while it runs; the core has rendered past stamp + 0.5 s and the gain reads 0.15 (4 decimals); the level never rises on the way down, takes at least 0.4 s of audio clock from first move to landing, and some reading lies strictly between; the Music meter falls below half its earlier peak; no quantum was split (`splitQuanta` unchanged); the second press gives a later stamp and the gain returns to 0.6.

### S2. A snapshot lands on one stamp. Key **4**, then **4** again

- **Press 4 and listen.** Correct (Underwater): over half a second the drums go **muffled** (the hats and the snare's hiss go first, then the whole kit sounds as if behind a door; low-pass 500 Hz) and **quieter** (0.6 x 0.6 = 0.36); the hum goes quieter and duller at the same moment (Ambience 0.5 and 900 Hz). Everything changes together: you should not hear the hum muffle before or after the drums.
- **Press 4 again.** Correct: the sound opens up again over half a second to exactly how it was.
- **A defect sounds like:** the drums getting quieter but not duller (no low-pass), or duller but not quieter; the hum and the drums changing at different instants; a click at the start or the end; the drums coming back darker or quieter than before (a lane that did not release).
- **Walker confirms:** a snapshot `BUS_RAMP` for Music (gain 0.6, low-pass 500 Hz), for Ambience (gain 0.5, 900 Hz) and for SFX (low-pass 1500 Hz), all with **one stamp** (the Music and Ambience rows show it), ahead of the core's frame; after stamp + 0.5 s Music reads 0.36, Ambience 0.4, SFX 1; the clear press sends Music's ramp back to gain 1 and the low-pass off (24000 Hz) on a new stamp, and Music and Ambience read 0.6 and 0.8 again. **Limit:** `Audio.debug()` carries no filter value for a bus, so the low-pass is checked in the record main sent, not in the sound: the muffling is for your ear.

### S3. A presence duck. Key **5**

- **Press 5 and listen.** Correct: a short noise burst (0.3 s). The drums **dip hard as it starts** (to 0.3 of their level, about 0.18 in all) in a few hundredths of a second, stay down while the burst sounds, then **swell back over about half a second** to full, taking a second or so to be fully back. The dip is smooth: no click, no pumping.
- **A defect sounds like:** no dip at all; the drums stepping down instead of sliding; the drums staying down after the burst, or coming back with a jump; a double dip (a second duck after the burst has ended).
- **Walker confirms:** Music's duck lane (`Audio.bus("Music").duckGain`, the core's own value) falls below 0.35 and not below 0.28 (the rule's 0.3); Music's effective gain falls below 0.2; no ducked reading before the key; a `BUS_PRESENCE` with SFX's bit went out and, when the burst ended, one without it; the lane returns to exactly 1.

### S4. A presence duck on the sound's own frame. Key **X**

This is the one that tells a stamped duck from an early or a late one.

- **Press X and listen.** Nothing happens for about a second. Then the burst sounds, and **the drums dip at the same instant**: the dip belongs to the burst's very first sample. Then it releases exactly as in S3.
- **A defect sounds like:** the drums dipping **before** the burst (a dip with nothing to explain it, up to a second early); the burst's first instant sounding over **full-level** drums and the dip arriving after it (late); a click at the dip.
- **Walker confirms:** the `BUS_PRESENCE` with SFX's bit is stamped between 0.8 and 1.5 s ahead of the core's frame at the key, and the burst's `PLAY` carries **the same frame**; every core status frame up to the quantum holding that frame reads the duck at exactly 1 and the SFX meter below 0.01 (nothing heard, nothing ducked before it); the first ducked reading and the first SFX meter reading both come after that quantum, the duck within 0.3 s of the stamp.

### S5. A muted bus's reverb send goes silent after its tail (post-fader). Keys **A**, **2**, **N**

- **Press A.** Correct: a steady broadband noise, with a room around it (the Ambience bus sends half its signal to the reverb), plus the hum.
- **Press 2** (mute Music) so only Ambience is left. Correct: the drums go quiet in one short ramp.
- **Press N** (mute Ambience). Correct: the **noise and the hum stop at once** (a very short fade, a few milliseconds), and **the room rings out over about two and a half seconds**, getting quieter smoothly, then **silence**. The tail is what was already in the reverb; nothing new feeds it, because the send follows the bus's fader.
- **Press N again.** Correct: the noise, the hum and the room come back, no click.
- **A defect sounds like:** after N, a quiet **hiss or wash that never stops** while the bus stays muted (the send is taking the signal before the fader: pre-fader, the old behaviour); the tail **chopped off** with a click or a thump when the bus mutes; a pop when you unmute.
- Then press **2** (Music back), **A** (noise off), **B** (drums off) and **P** (the tone back) to leave the page as you found it.
- **Walker confirms:** with Music muted, the master meter reads above 0.02 for Ambience alone; after N Ambience's effective gain reads 0 and its voices read `busMuted`; the master meter still reads above 0.005 within 0.6 s of the mute (the tail rings out, it is not cut), and **below 0.002 from 4 s on** (post-fader: silent, measured over audio-clock time); after the second N Ambience reads 0.8 and the master meter is back above 0.02.

### S6. The owner's way with the keys: late, twice, held

You press at human pace, and not always at a good moment. These are the cases the headless walker also runs in its `M5-0 (listen mode)` case; do each once by ear.

- **A late key.** Leave the page alone (drums on) for about 12 seconds, then press **3**. Correct: it sounds exactly like S1, with no lurch at the start: the page sitting idle does not change how a fade starts. A defect: the first fade after a long idle starts with a jump, or noticeably later than usual.
- **A key twice.** Press **3**, and press **3** again about a quarter of a second later, in the middle of the fade. Correct: the level turns around **from wherever it had got to** and rises to full, with no click and no jump to either end. The page ends at 0.6. A defect: a jump to the low level or to full at the second press; ending at the wrong level.
- **A key held.** Hold **3** down for a second (the keyboard repeats). Correct: **one** fade, down, once; releasing does nothing. A defect: the fade flipping up and down while you hold. Press **3** once more to bring it back.
- **Mute at human pace, twice.** Do S5's N, wait for the silence, N to unmute; do it again after a pause. Correct both times as in S5.
- **Walker confirms (listen-mode case):** the late key's stamp is ahead of the frame the key saw and within 0.75 s of it, and the fade lands on its length; the double press sends two ramps on Music's runtime lane in order, gain 0.25 then 1, the second stamped no earlier than the first, and the gain ends at 0.6; the held key sends exactly one ramp (gain 0.25); the mute is silent after its tail both rounds.

## What the walker cannot do

- **Hear.** Headless Chromium has no sound output: every "correct" and "defect" sentence above about how it sounds (a click, a zipper, muffling, a wash) is yours. The walker reads lane values, the duck, the records main sent and the core's meters, so it proves the step ran as built and which frame the change was stamped for.
- **Clicks and zipper, objectively.** The sample-to-sample jump (`maxStep`, F-40) of the M5-0 scenes is the discontinuity check in the goldens (task DS0), not this section.
- **The snapshot's low-pass** is read from the record main sent, not from the sound (`Audio.debug()` has no per-bus filter value).
- **True latency.** "A fraction of a second" is a stamp ahead of the clock, not a measurement of key to ear.
